## 4. The artwork must be a work of art

This is the most important acceptance principle.

The finished poster must be an independently meaningful piece of visual art. It must not look like:

- a generic promotional poster;
- a generic AI music poster or a generic AI-generated album cover;
- a Canva template;
- an image with a title simply placed over it;
- a motivational design;
- a visually attractive but conceptually empty composition.

The generated visual, typography, negative space, texture, material and emotional message must work together as one composition. Every element must have a creative purpose. Do not add elements to make the poster look more expensive.

**Typography layout.** The designer determines the actual text positions only after the standalone visual is approved (Gate 2). The layout must respond to the filament's path, the thread-to-ash transition, the ash tip, the empty ground, the shadows and the overall emotional balance. The title must feel integrated, not placed in a predetermined rectangular area. Any composition guidance in the production brief is starting guidance for generation and does not dictate the typography layout.

## 7. Mandatory artwork acceptance tests

All six tests must be completed on **actual generated artwork.** Never perform them on placeholders, mock-ups or unfinished stand-ins. Do not mark any test as passed before its evidence exists.

**Evidence for each test**

| Test | Evidence it depends on | Step |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A. First impression | **Audience evidence** (viewers' first impressions) | 9, after audience testing at step 8 |
| B. Standalone artwork quality | **Designer evaluation** of the text-free generated visual | Preliminary at step 3, final at step 6 |
| C. Meaning | **Audience evidence** (the emotion or message viewers perceived) | 9, after step 8 |
| D. Visual distinctiveness | **Designer evaluation** of the actual visual | 9 |
| E. Typography | **Designer evaluation** of the actual typography on the master artwork | 9 |
| F. Memorability | **Audience evidence** (what viewers remembered) | 9, after step 8 |

Tests A, C and F **cannot be assessed or marked complete before audience evidence exists.** The designer's own impression is recorded separately and never substitutes for it. Designer assessment and audience feedback are always reported separately.

**Test B must evaluate the generated visual without any typography,** and it is mandatory at two stages:

| Stage | Applied to | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Preliminary Test B** | The text-free outputs of the three explorations, and the selected exploration (step 3) | Part of the selection decision, before Gate 1 |
| **Final Test B** | The refined, text-free standalone visual (step 6) | Part of Gate 2 |

Passing preliminary Test B on an exploration does **not** mean the refined standalone visual passes final Test B. A visual that works only after typography is added must be marked **INCOMPLETE.** It must be a meaningful, compelling piece of art on its own.

| Test | Question | Pass condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A. First impression** | Does the artwork make someone stop and look? | Viewers' recorded first impressions show they stop and look, shown without any explanation. |
| **B. Standalone artwork quality** | Remove all text, wordmarks and promotional information. Does the generated visual remain an intentional, compelling piece of art? | Strong, intentional composition. Emotional impression without text. A meaningful relationship between the intact filament, the ash transition and the empty space. Compelling on its own. ## 8. Independent audience testing (mandatory, step 8)

Use **5–8 independent viewers** who have not been told the intended meaning. Show them the master artwork (ASHBORN and MARATHON only) at step 8, after it exists and before Tests A, C and F are completed.

**Protocol**
- **Consistent viewing conditions.** Every viewer sees the artwork on the same kind of display, at the same size, with the same viewing time and the same instructions. The exact values are **Provisional** and must be decided and recorded before the first viewer.
- **No concept explanation before viewing.** Do not describe the idea, the emotional territory or the creative direction.
- **No promotional context before viewing.** No release information, streaming information, artist background or song description.
- **No competing explorations shown before the initial responses.** Show only the artwork under test.
- **Ask exactly these three questions, in order,** and do not prompt or comment:
  1. What was your first impression?
  2. What emotion or message did you perceive?
  3. What do you remember after viewing it?
- **Record responses accurately,** verbatim where possible, before any discussion.
- **Do not influence responses.** If a viewer asks how the image was made, answer honestly after their responses are recorded.
- **Report audience feedback separately from the designer's assessment.**

**This is qualitative feedback, not statistical validation.** Five to eight responses show how a few viewers reacted. They do not prove how people in general will react.

## 9. Contrast and readability

Internal ASHBORN artwork acceptance criteria. They are **not** formal WCAG certification. The contrast formula borrows the WCAG relative-luminance definition, and nothing here claims WCAG conformance. The thresholds are unchanged.

**When:** contrast testing takes place **after the final typography is placed**, on the actual final text positions.

**Where:** every final exported artwork format that contains the relevant text.

| Text element | Where it appears | Average pixel-level contrast | Worst 1% of pixel-level contrast |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| MARATHON | Master artwork and promotional adaptations | at least 7:1 | at least 4.5:1 |
| ASHBORN wordmark | Master artwork and promotional adaptations | reported, no minimum | at least 4.5:1 |
| DEBUT SINGLE — OUT NOW | Promotional adaptations, where the descriptor is used | reported, no minimum | at least 4.5:1 |

**Measurement rules**
- Use the background-only visual (the approved visual without text) with each text element at its actual final position.
- The **actual glyph footprint** (pixels under the real glyph shapes) determines pass or fail.
- The **expanded 4 px footprint** is measured and reported separately. It is diagnostic only and does not independently decide pass or fail.
- Report, for each footprint: average pixel-level contrast, worst 1% of pixel-level results, measured text colour and measured background colours.
- Never calculate contrast from the average colour of a rectangular text area.
- Take the text colour from the final artwork, not from the specification.
- Test final exports at their actual dimensions, and inspect appropriately downscaled previews for readability after resizing and compression.
- If a text element fails, first move the text or adjust the visual. A neutral tonal scrim is a last resort, needs ASHBORN's approval, and must not look like a box, a visible gradient or a decorative effect.

### 9.1 Measurement methodology (define before the contrast script is implemented)

The script must not be implemented until this methodology is approved. **Details marked PROVISIONAL are proposals, not decisions.**

| # | Requirement | Method | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Glyph-mask generation** | Render the text layer alone on a transparent background at the export's exact dimensions, using the same renderer and settings as the export. Take each pixel's coverage α from that render. The **glyph footprint** is the set of pixels with α ≥ 0.5 | PROVISIONAL (the α cut-off) |
| 2 | **Anti-aliasing treatment** | Use grayscale anti-aliasing, with sub-pixel (LCD) rendering off. Pixels with 0 < α < 0.5 are excluded from the glyph footprint. They fall inside the 4 px diagnostic footprint | PROVISIONAL |
| 3 | **Text opacity and blending** | Record the opacity and blend mode of every text element. Measure the effective text colour from the final export, as the median of pixels with α = 1 in each text run (per run, so the two ASHBORN tones are measured separately). Never use the specification colour | PROVISIONAL |
| 4 | **Background pixel pairing** | Produce a background-only render with the same pipeline and the text layer removed, at identical dimensions and with no resampling. Pair each footprint pixel with the background-only pixel at the same x, y. A scrim, if any, is part of the background-only render | PROVISIONAL |
| 5 | **Worst 1% calculation** | For each footprint pixel compute the contrast ratio (L1 + 0.05) / (L2 + 0.05) between the effective text colour and the paired background pixel, using sRGB relative luminance. **Average** = the arithmetic mean of the per-pixel ratios. **Worst 1%** = the 1st percentile of the per-pixel ratios (nearest rank). Also report the minimum and the mean of the lowest 1% as diagnostics. Pass or fail uses the 1st percentile | PROVISIONAL (percentile definition) |
| 6 | **Very small footprints** | Report the pixel count n for every footprint. If n < 100, the 1st percentile is not stable, so the worst 1% value is the single lowest ratio. If n < 30, report the values and flag them as insufficient for a statistical reading | PROVISIONAL (the cut-offs) |
| 7 | **Final exported images** | Measure the exact lossless file that will be published. For a lossy deliverable (for example JPEG or WebP), measure the decoded file against a background-only file exported with identical settings, and record the difference from the lossless result as a diagnostic. The thresholds apply to the published file | PROVISIONAL |
| 8 | **Browser-rendered text (website)** | Render each layout in a headless browser at defined viewports and device pixel ratios, after fonts have loaded and layout is stable. Take a background-only screenshot (text made transparent) and a mask screenshot (text white on black with identical layout), then proceed as in rows 1–7. Record the browser name and version, operating system, viewport, device pixel ratio, font files, CSS and hero image source. **The viewports and ratios to test are Pending** | PROVISIONAL |
| 9 | **Repeatability and evidence logging** | The measurement is scripted and deterministic: the same inputs give the same results. Pin tool versions. Keep a configuration file. Log SHA-256 checksums of every input, the script version, the parameters, the date, and per-element results (pixel count, average, 1st percentile, minimum, text colours, brightest 1%, darkest 1% and average background colours, footprint, pass or fail). Save machine-readable results and annotated diagnostic images in the records folder. Validate the script on synthetic images of known contrast, which are test fixtures and not artwork, before relying on it | PROVISIONAL |

Where a measurement detail is not listed above, it is undecided. Mark it PROVISIONAL rather than inventing an answer.

## 10. AI provenance and terms (acceptance requirement)

The production brief defines the records and checkpoints in detail. For acceptance, the following must hold:

- **Checkpoint 1, before generation:** the platform, model and plan are recorded. Intended commercial use, plan restrictions, prompt and upload retention, training and data-use provisions, and output watermarking and provenance have been checked. A dated copy of the applicable terms is saved.
- **Checkpoint 2, before commercial release:** the commercial-use terms, plan conditions, output ownership provisions, any changes since checkpoint 1, and required disclosure and provenance have been rechecked. A new dated copy is saved.
- A generation record exists with: platform and model, generation date, exact prompts, original outputs, the selected output, refinement history, human modifications and final export files.
- Never claim AI-generated artwork is a real photograph or entirely human-generated.
- No promise of exclusive copyright protection is made merely because an AI platform assigns output rights, and contractual commercial-use permission is never described as establishing exclusive copyright ownership.
- The wording for disclosing AI use is decided (**Pending**).

### 11.1 Resolving "functional website" against "untouched until approval"

Two instructions looked contradictory: the website must be functional and accessible even while final artwork awaits approval, and the website source must stay untouched until the master artwork passes its approval gate. They are resolved as follows.

- **The gate wins on timing.** No website source is created or modified until the final artwork approval (section 12). That approval is the master artwork approval gate. Nothing is built, and no placeholder is used to get ahead of it.
- **"Functional and accessible" is a requirement on the eventual build,** not permission to build earlier. When step 12 begins, the page must work and stay accessible whether or not a hero image has loaded: working streaming links, readable text, semantic structure and sensible behaviour if an image fails or is slow. It must not depend on the artwork to be usable.
- **The page never fills the gap with fake artwork.** No gradient, shape or illustration stands in for the artwork. The treatment of the page when an image is unavailable is **Pending** and is not presented as artwork.
- **The situation of building around unapproved artwork cannot arise,** because the build starts only after approval. If the artwork is ever withdrawn after the build begins, the owner decides what happens next.
- **Update, 2026-10-30:** the owner lifted the gate for the landing page and approved the poster in section 3.4 as its hero. The build may begin around that poster. The rules above that forbid placeholders and substitutes still apply.

### 11.2 Landing-page visuals: acceptance rules

Landing-page visuals are the hero images and any additional campaign visuals made for the website, including the separate social-sharing image.

- They are original and made for this release, never derived from the rejected existing cover (section 3.3).
- They are consistent with the approved master artwork and use its approved palette, typography, wordmark and rules.

- They carry the production brief's provenance, terms and records requirements, and are never presented as a photograph or as entirely human-made.
- **A separate landing-page visual exploration round takes place after Gate 2** (owner decision, 2026-10-30). It is not E1, E2 or E3, is not a master exploration, and uses the landing-page direction in section 3.2. Its content, the number of candidates and its exact position between Gate 2 and step 12 are **Pending.** The twelve-step sequence in section 13 is unchanged.
- **Tests A, C and F apply to landing-page visuals where appropriate** (owner decision, 2026-10-30), using the audience protocol in section 8 and recording audience evidence separately from the designer's assessment. Contrast applies wherever text sits on the image. These are Provisional and are not Tests A, C or F.
- A visual cannot be used on the website until the owner approves it explicitly, and the website cannot begin before the final artwork approval.
