## 3. Original conceptual art direction

**The visual.** A delicate filament changes from intact thread into a fragile, ash-like structure that has kept its form. It should communicate emotional pain, fragility, something lost and something that remains, and a personal journey that is difficult to explain. The emotional experience must come before the title.

**Mandatory visual characteristics** (apply to every exploration)
- One recognisable, continuous natural cotton filament. It never loses its structural continuity.
- An intact pale cotton structure that transitions gradually into charcoal-grey, ash-like fibres, with the original twisting structure still visible during the transformation.
- Physically believable fibre behaviour.
- A warm, mineral-textured environment.
- A warm, desaturated palette of taupe, mineral grey, charcoal and bone.
- Natural directional side lighting.
- Meaningful negative space and a sophisticated asymmetry.
- No disconnected, unrelated fibres that break the continuity of the original filament.
- No artificial glow, no flames, smoke or embers, no people or hands, no additional narrative objects, and no lettering, text or watermark.

**Composition is set by each exploration (section 6), not fixed here.** Orientation of the filament, framing, lighting angle and focal-point placement may differ between explorations. The filament must stay legible at phone-preview size, judged during evaluation.

**Meaning guard.** Do not depict people, events, letters, photographs, jewellery or names. The meaning must be emotionally understandable without being over-explained, leaving room for interpretation.

**Generic AI tells to reject.** Glowing particles or floating dust, lens flares and rim glow, saturated teal and orange grading, a centred symmetrical "epic" hero shot, over-smooth plastic surfaces, impossible thread geometry or repeating fibre patterns, fake bokeh, over-sharpened HDR rendering, stray objects, garbled text, and dramatic smoke or embers.

**Originality guard.** A strong result must be recognisably this concept and not simply "the output of a prompt". Before selection, ask what in the image could only belong to ASHBORN — MARATHON. Check candidates for close resemblance to existing artwork with a reverse-image search. **Never use the names of living artists or existing artwork as prompt references.**

## 6. Prompt development

**Anatomy of a prompt:** subject and material · structure · composition and negative space · light · palette · texture · atmosphere · technical parameters (aspect ratio, resolution, seed where supported). **Negative constraints are kept separate from the positive prompt.**

**Vocabulary.** Use "carbonised" and "ash-like fibres that keep the twist". Avoid words that tend to produce flames or drama ("burnt", "fire", "ember", "smoke") in the positive prompt, and place them in the negative constraints.

**Aspect ratio.** Generate the explorations at portrait 2:3 (Provisional), the conceptual master ratio. If the platform has no 2:3 option, record the nearest available ratio. Any later change of ratio is an adaptation (section 10).

**Draft positive base prompt (Provisional. Not yet run. Adapt to the chosen platform's syntax).** It carries the material and emotional requirements and sets no orientation or position:

> A single continuous natural cotton filament, twisted, lying on a warm, mineral-textured surface. One part of the filament is intact and pale bone-white, with visible twisted cotton structure and fine fibre fuzz. Along its length the same filament transitions gradually into charcoal-grey, ash-like fibres that keep the original twist and the continuity of the filament. Physically believable fibres. Natural directional side lighting with soft shadows. Meaningful negative space and a sophisticated asymmetrical composition. A warm, desaturated palette of taupe, mineral grey, charcoal and bone. Quiet, still, intimate. Realistic matte material texture.

**Each exploration adds its own composition clause** (section 7 defines the explorations, and the clause is added to the base prompt):

| Exploration | Composition clause (Provisional) |
| --- | --- |
| E1 The Unravelling | A close, intimate material study. The filament occupies a significant part of the composition. Intricate cotton fibre structure, visible twisting, fraying and gradual carbonisation. The physical material quality is the focal point |
| E2 The Absence | A small, delicate filament within an expansive mineral environment. Generous negative space and an asymmetrical composition with restrained natural lighting. The surrounding empty environment is an important compositional element |
| E3 What Remains | The transition between cotton and ash is the main focal point, and the original filament structure remains recognisable. Intricate charcoal-grey fibres. A distinctive asymmetrical composition with strong material contrast and no artificial effects |

**Negative prompt (Provisional, kept separate):** flames, fire, smoke, embers, sparks, glow, floating particles, bokeh, lens flare, disconnected or unrelated loose fibres, scattered debris, text, lettering, watermark, people, hands, extra objects, saturated colour, strict symmetry, plastic or glossy look, hyper-sharp HDR.

**Prompt rules**
- Do not force the same arrangement, lighting, orientation, framing or focal-point placement on every exploration. Each exploration's clause establishes its own composition.
- Never name living artists or existing artwork in a prompt. This includes the existing MARATHON cover: never upload it, describe it or reference it in a prompt.
- Do not put lyric text in a prompt in a way that could place lettering in the image. Any lyric-derived element is the owner's explicit instruction and is recorded (section 2.1). The model never supplies or invents lyrics.
- Change one variable per run, and keep the seed where the platform supports it.
- Never overwrite or delete an original output.
- Log every run in the generation record before looking at the next result.

Select the strongest. Record why it was chosen and why the others were not. **Gate 1: explicit selection approval.**

## 8. Image refinement (no typography)

**Allowed, each step logged with its input and output:** upscaling with a recorded tool · local repair of defects by inpainting · extending the canvas to create negative space · subtle tonal and colour adjustment toward the palette targets · consistent grain · removing stray artefacts.

**Not allowed:** adding flames, smoke, glow or objects · compositing unrelated or stock imagery · adding text · heavy filters or flat colour overlays · fake lens effects.

**Resolution.** The conceptual master is **portrait 2:3, with a proposed working resolution of 2160 × 3240 (Provisional).** The final canvas and resolution of the master artwork are **Pending** (section 10). AI platforms often generate at a lower native size, so **upscaling will probably be needed.** It must be recorded, and quality must be judged at 100%. Do not claim native pixels that do not exist.

**Orientations and ratios.** 2:3 is not 9:16. **Do not stretch or automatically crop the master.** Landscape and other ratios are composition-specific adaptations (section 10). Any new generation or substantial regeneration, including a re-generation to obtain another ratio, is a **new candidate:** it gets its own run ID, is recorded, and needs its own approval.

**Gate 2: standalone visual approval.** Before it, run final Test B on the refined, text-free visual and confirm it is suitable for the complete composition. Passing preliminary Test B does not carry over.

### 11.1 Palette verification procedure

The approved targets stay as written in section 2. They are **not** converted into flat colour overlays, and natural lighting and material variation must be preserved.

1. **Identify representative regions** of the actual artwork for each target: lit ground, shade, intact thread, scorched transition, char and ash. Record how each region was chosen (mask or coordinates). Avoid edges and cast shadows that belong to another region.
2. **Sample representative pixels** from each region. Provisional: take the median of the pixels in the region on the exported file, and record the number of pixels used.
3. **Record the measured colour values** as sRGB hex and as CIELAB.
4. **Compare against the relevant target** using **CIEDE2000** (ΔE00) as the perceptual colour difference. Convert sRGB to CIELAB with a stated white point (provisionally D65), and validate the implementation against published CIEDE2000 reference pairs before relying on it.
5. **Record deviations.** Report ΔE00 together with the differences in lightness, chroma and hue, so it is clear what drifted.
6. **Distinguish intentional lighting variation from unwanted colour drift.** A deviation mostly in lightness that follows the light fall-off across a region is lighting variation. A hue or chroma shift across the whole image, or across one material, is drift. Record the judgement and its reasoning.

**No universal numerical pass threshold is set,** because none has been justified. ΔE00 is recorded as evidence, and the owner decides whether a deviation is acceptable. Any numerical threshold must be justified and approved separately and is **Pending.**
