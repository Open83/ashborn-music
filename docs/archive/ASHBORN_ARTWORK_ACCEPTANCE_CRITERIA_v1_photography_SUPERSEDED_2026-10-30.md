# ASHBORN — MARATHON: Artwork Acceptance Criteria

**Internal creative acceptance document.** For the ASHBORN project owner, the creative team and anyone who reviews or approves the final artwork. It is not a photographer instruction document.

- **Last updated:** 2026-10-30 (final documentation refinement and lock)
- **Documentation status: LOCKED** for the current MARATHON release direction. Future changes require a specific reason and explicit approval.
- **Nothing in this document records a test as passed.** Every result field is empty until real photographic artwork has been evaluated.

## 3. Approved artistic vision

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Artist | ASHBORN |
| Release | MARATHON (debut single) |
| Creative concept | **FILAMENT — WHAT REMAINS** |
| Central idea | Something may have been lost, but something remains. |

**Emotional territory:** emotional pain · personal journeys · loss and memories · fragility · internal struggles · what remains after difficult experiences.

This is an artistic interpretation. It is not an assumed explanation of the song's lyrics or personal story. Do not invent lyrics, personal experiences or a literal narrative. The meaning should be emotionally understandable without being over-explained, and viewers should have room to interpret it.

## 4. The artwork must be a work of art

This is the most important acceptance principle.

The finished poster must be an independently meaningful piece of visual art. It must not look like:

- a generic promotional poster;
- a Canva template;
- a photograph with a title simply placed over it;
- a motivational design;
- a generic AI-generated album cover;
- a visually attractive but conceptually empty composition.

The photograph, typography, negative space, texture, material and emotional message must work together as one composition. Every element must have a creative purpose. Do not add elements to make the poster look more expensive.

**Typography layout.** The designer determines the actual text positions only after the master photograph is approved (Gate 2). The layout must respond to the filament's path, the thread-to-ash transition, the ash tip, the empty ground, the shadows and the overall emotional balance. The title must feel integrated, not placed in a predetermined rectangular area. The Frame P and Frame L percentages and the lighting targets in the photography brief are photographic planning specifications only. They do not dictate the typography layout.

## 7. Mandatory artwork acceptance tests

All six tests must be completed on **actual photographic artwork**. Never perform them on fabricated photographs, placeholders or unfinished mock-ups.

**Test B is mandatory at two stages:**

| Stage | Applied to | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Preliminary Test B** | The actual test-day photographs | Part of Gate 1. A preliminary check on artistic quality |
| **Final Test B** | The actual master photograph, after the main shoot | Part of Gate 2 |

Passing preliminary Test B on a test-day photograph does **not** mean the final master photograph passes Test B. A photograph that works only after typography is added must be marked **INCOMPLETE**.

| Test | Question | Pass condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A. First impression** | Does the artwork make someone stop and look? | Reviewers stop and look, shown without any explanation. |
| **B. Standalone artwork quality** | Remove all text, wordmarks and promotional information. Does the actual photograph remain an intentional, compelling piece of art? | Strong, intentional composition. Emotional impression without text. A meaningful relationship between the intact filament, the ash transition and the empty space. Compelling on its own. Does not look like a generic macro photograph. **If it only works after typography is added, mark it INCOMPLETE.** |
| **C. Meaning** | Can viewers perceive an emotional idea without being told the intended interpretation? | Viewers describe an emotional idea without prompting. |
| **D. Visual distinctiveness** | Does the composition have its own distinctive visual identity? | Judge artistic distinctiveness and memorability only. Do not claim viewers can recognise ASHBORN from one image without prior exposure. Distinctiveness is not established brand recognition. |
| **E. Typography** | Do the wordmark and title strengthen the artwork rather than compete with it? | Typography and image read as one composition, not separate layers. |
| **F. Memorability** | After a brief viewing, is there a distinctive visual element people remember? | Viewers name a specific element unprompted. |

For each test, record: **result, evidence, weaknesses, required corrections, approval status.**

## 8. Independent audience testing

Use **5–8 independent viewers** who have not been told the intended meaning. Do not explain the concept before showing the artwork, and do not influence responses. Use the same display conditions for every viewer.

Ask exactly these three questions, in order:

1. What was your first impression?
2. What emotion or message did you perceive?
3. What do you remember after viewing it?

Record responses accurately and verbatim where possible. **Report audience feedback separately from the designer's assessment.** This is qualitative feedback, not statistical validation.

## 9. Contrast and readability

Internal ASHBORN artwork acceptance criteria. They are **not** formal WCAG certification.

**When:** contrast testing takes place **after the final typography is placed**, on the actual final text positions. It does not take place on test-day photographs or on provisional text positions.

**Where:** every final exported artwork format that contains the relevant text.

| Text element | Where it appears | Average pixel-level contrast | Worst 1% of pixel-level contrast |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| MARATHON | Master artwork and promotional adaptations | at least 7:1 | at least 4.5:1 |
| ASHBORN wordmark | Master artwork and promotional adaptations | reported, no minimum | at least 4.5:1 |
| DEBUT SINGLE — OUT NOW | Promotional adaptations, where the descriptor is used | reported, no minimum | at least 4.5:1 |

**Measurement rules**
- Use the background-only photograph with each text element at its actual final position.
- The **actual glyph footprint** (pixels under the real glyph shapes) determines pass or fail.
- The **expanded 4 px footprint** is measured and reported separately. It is diagnostic only and does not independently decide pass or fail.
- Report, for each footprint: average pixel-level contrast, worst 1% of pixel-level results, measured text colour and measured background colours.
- Never calculate contrast from the average colour of a rectangular text area.
- Take the text colour from the final artwork, not from the specification.
- Test final exports at their actual dimensions, and inspect appropriately downscaled previews for readability after resizing and compression.
- If a text element fails, first move the text or fix the lighting. A neutral tonal scrim is a last resort, needs ASHBORN's approval, and must not look like a box, a visible gradient or a decorative effect. Natural photographic lighting and tonal fall-off come first.

| Format | Size | Text present | Measured | Result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Instagram Story | 1080 × 1920 | Per version exported | No | Not tested |
| WhatsApp Status | 1080 × 1920 | Per version exported | No | Not tested |
| Instagram Square | 1080 × 1080 | Per version exported | No | Not tested |
| Open Graph | 1200 × 630 | Per version exported | No | Not tested |
| Desktop website hero | Live layout | Per final layout | No | Not tested |
| Mobile website hero | Live layout | Per final layout | No | Not tested |

The thresholds are applied to every final exported format that contains the relevant text. The descriptor row applies only where the descriptor appears.

## 11. Approval gates

### Gate 1: test photograph approval (after the photographic test day)

Explicit approval is required before the main shoot. Review:

- the actual test photographs;
- technical feasibility;
- preliminary artistic quality;
- **preliminary Test B**;
- confirmation that the materials, lighting and composition can be reproduced reliably.

**The main shoot cannot proceed without explicit Gate 1 approval.**

### Gate 2: master photograph approval (after the main shoot)

Explicit approval is required before any artwork design begins. Review:

- all final photographic deliverables;
- selection of the master portrait and landscape photographs;
- image quality and required framing;
- **final Test B**, evaluating the final photograph as standalone artwork;
- confirmation that the photograph is suitable for the complete artistic composition.

**No master artwork design and no website implementation begins before Gate 2 approval.**

### Final artwork approval (after artwork development)

The master poster cannot receive final approval until **all** of the following are complete. If anything is incomplete, report it honestly. Do not artificially increase scores or approve weak artwork.

- [ ] Gate 1 and Gate 2 were both approved explicitly
- [ ] Tests A–F have been evaluated and recorded in section 7.1
- [ ] Final Test B passes on the master photograph
- [ ] Independent audience feedback has been collected (section 8)
- [ ] Typography (typeface, wordmark tracking and tone) is approved on the real master photograph
- [ ] Contrast requirements pass on the actual glyph footprint in every final exported format (section 9)
- [ ] The complete composition communicates a meaningful visual idea
- [ ] The artwork demonstrates originality and artistic quality
- [ ] All required adaptations maintain the visual identity
- [ ] The final text layout responds to the photograph and is not placed in a predetermined box
- [ ] The master artwork carries only ASHBORN and MARATHON
- [ ] The promotional adaptations carry DEBUT SINGLE — OUT NOW and streaming information without compromising the composition
